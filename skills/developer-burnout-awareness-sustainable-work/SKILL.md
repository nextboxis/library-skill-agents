# Developer Burnout Awareness & Sustainable Pacing

## When to use
Use this skill when observing signs of chronic exhaustion, cynical detachment, declining velocity, late-night commit surges, or emotional volatility in colleagues, direct reports, or yourself.

## Core principles
- **Burnout is an Organizational & Systemic Injury**: Burnout is not personal weakness or lack of grit; it is the physiological consequence of prolonged chronic workplace stress without recovery.
- **The Three Hallmarks of Burnout (Maslach Inventory)**:
  - *Exhaustion*: Deep, unshakeable physical and cognitive fatigue.
  - *Cynicism & Depersonalization*: Feeling bitter toward the project, company, or users; checked-out detachment.
  - *Inefficacy*: Feeling that no matter how much code you ship, nothing matters or improves.
- **The False Economy of Heroics**: 80-hour sprint weeks produce sloppy, buggy code that creates technical debt and production incidents, ultimately slowing long-term delivery.
- **Rest is a Professional Obligation**: High-cognitive engineering requires a rested prefrontal cortex; cognitive replenishment is as essential as unit tests.

## Step-by-step workflow
1. **Identify behavioral warning signals**: Notice 2:00 AM pull requests, missed standups, irritability in PR reviews, or expressions of despair.
2. **Initiate a private, non-judgmental check-in**: Approach with compassionate curiosity rather than performance criticism.
3. **Validate the unsustainable load**: Explicitly acknowledge that the current pace, on-call burden, or feature timeline is unrealistic.
4. **Enforce immediate tactical boundaries**:
   - Cancel non-critical meetings.
   - Reassign incoming alerts or on-call shifts.
   - Push back project deadlines with stakeholders.
5. **Establish sustainable recovery rhythms**: Encourage complete disconnection during weekends, regular PTO, and sane working hours.

## Behavioral guidelines & dialogue examples
- **Dialogue Example (Empathetic 1:1 Check-in)**:
  - *Lead / Peer*: "Hey Alex, I noticed you were committing fixes at 3:00 AM on Sunday, and you seemed really exhausted during retrospective. You've carried an incredible load keeping the billing migration alive, but I'm worried you are burning the candle at both ends. How are you genuinely holding up?"
  - *Engineer*: "Honestly? I'm exhausted. Every time I close an issue, three more appear. I feel like if I stop working for an hour, the whole release slips and everyone blames me."
  - *Sustainable Response*: "Thank you for being honest with me. No single feature or release date is worth your physical or mental health. The release date was based on an estimate, not a law of physics. Starting today, I am taking you off the on-call rotation for the next two weeks, and we are going to push the non-critical milestones to next sprint. I want you to log off at 5:30 PM today, and we will protect that boundary together."

## Common mistakes & pitfalls
- Celebrating "hero culture" and rewarding engineers who sleep under desks or answer Slack at 2:00 AM.
- Suggesting surface wellness band-aids (like meditation apps or pizza parties) instead of addressing workload and staffing issues.
- Treating burnout as a personal time-management flaw rather than a structural issue.

## Verification checklist
- [ ] Warning signs of chronic exhaustion and cynicism are recognized early.
- [ ] Engineering conversations actively discourage heroic overwork.
- [ ] Practical workload adjustments (shifting dates, reassigning on-call) are enacted.
- [ ] Disconnection boundaries (evenings, weekends, PTO) are respected and defended.
