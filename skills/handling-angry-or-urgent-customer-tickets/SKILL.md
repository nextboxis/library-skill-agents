# Handling Angry or Urgent Customer Support Tickets

## When to use
Use this skill when responding to tier-3 escalated support tickets, priority enterprise client emergencies, angry executive escalations, or public customer complaints on social channels.

## Core principles
- **Urgency Demonstrates Respect**: When a customer's business is halted, slow responses feel like contempt. Speed of acknowledgment matters as much as speed of resolution.
- **The Customer is Scared, Not Just Angry**: Rage in enterprise B2B support almost always stems from fear: fear of looking incompetent to their boss, fear of losing client contracts, or fear of audit failure.
- **Radical Accountability**: Never blame internal departments ("Our billing team messed up"), third-party vendors ("AWS had an issue"), or the user ("You misconfigured it"). The customer hired your company as a unified entity.
- **Actionable Timelines Beat Vague Assurances**: "We are working on it" causes anxiety. "Senior engineer Priya is reviewing the transaction logs; our next update will be at 2:30 PM EST" provides psychological security.

## Step-by-step workflow
1. **Absorb the full complaint**: Read the entire ticket without skimming or defensive dismissiveness.
2. **Send immediate high-priority acknowledgment**: Reply within 10–15 minutes acknowledging the business disruption.
3. **Validate the frustration and express ownership**: State unequivocally that their downtime or broken workflow is a top priority.
4. **Outline the immediate operational roadmap**: Explain the exact steps being taken to diagnose and resolve the issue.
5. **Provide a hard timestamp for the next update**: Commit to a specific time for the next status check-in, and honor it even if the update is only incremental.

## Behavioral guidelines & dialogue examples
- **Dialogue Example (Enterprise Customer Ticket Escalation)**:
  - *Customer Ticket*: "URGENT SEV-1: Our payroll export has been failing for 4 hours. 2,000 employees won't get paid tomorrow morning! Your support chat gave us generic canned responses. If this isn't fixed today we are terminating our $500k ARR contract immediately!"
  - *Mastery Response*:
    "Dear Mark,
    
    I completely understand the severity of this situation: failing to deliver payroll to 2,000 employees tomorrow morning is an existential business emergency, and you have every right to be furious about the generic responses you received earlier.
    
    I have escalated this ticket to our Emergency Engineering Response group. Our Principal Database Architect, Carlos, is actively tracing the failed payroll export job (Job ID #88412). We have identified an unexpected timeout in the batch currency conversion worker.
    
    We are running a manual batch retry with extended timeouts right now. I will provide our next direct status update at 3:15 PM EST (in 25 minutes), or the moment the batch completes. You have my direct contact details below."

## Common mistakes & pitfalls
- Copy-pasting boilerplate canned replies ("Thank you for contacting support! Your ticket is very important to us").
- Arguing over whether the issue qualifies as a "Severity 1" according to the contract SLA while the customer's system is down.
- Going dark for hours while working on a fix, leaving the customer in agonizing silence.

## Verification checklist
- [ ] Ticket acknowledgment is immediate, personalized, and urgent.
- [ ] Customer's business stakes (payroll, revenue, deadlines) are explicitly acknowledged.
- [ ] Clear diagnostic steps and lead engineer assignments are communicated.
- [ ] A concrete, non-negotiable timestamp for the next update is provided.
