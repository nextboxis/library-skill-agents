# Pomodoro Technique & Energy Management for Developers

## When to use
Use this skill when struggling with low energy, procrastination, high cognitive fatigue, repetitive refactoring tasks, or when easing into difficult technical assignments.

## Core principles
- **Energy Management Trumps Time Management**: You have plenty of time, but limited daily cognitive energy. Structuring work into paced sprint-and-recovery cycles protects mental stamina.
- **The Classical Pomodoro Cadence (Francesco Cirillo)**:
  - 25 minutes of single-task focus.
  - 5 minutes of mandatory physical rest (no screens).
  - Repeat 4 cycles, followed by a longer 20–30 minute break.
- **The Developer-Adjusted 50/10 Variant**: For complex engineering tasks where 25 minutes is too short to load mental models, a **50-minute sprint / 10-minute rest** cadence provides optimal immersion and recovery.
- **The Break is Non-Negotiable**: Staring at Twitter or Reddit during breaks does not restore cognitive stamina; true rest requires looking away from screens, stretching, or hydrating.

## Step-by-step workflow
1. **Select the single task**: Pick one specific ticket, bug, or module to work on.
2. **Set timer for sprint interval**: 25 minutes (classic) or 50 minutes (developer variant).
3. **Work with fierce singular focus**: Touch zero notifications, close external tabs, and write code until the timer rings.
4. **Mandatory rest when timer sounds**: Step away from the computer immediately. Do not finish "just one more line".
5. **Track completed pomodoros**: Log completed sprints to visualize progress and build momentum.

## Behavioral guidelines & dialogue examples
- **What Real Developer Rest Looks Like (The 5/10-Minute Break)**:
  - *Fake Rest (Cognitive Exhaustion)*: Browsing Hacker News, checking email, reading technical Slack debates.
  - *Real Rest (Cognitive Renewal)*: Standing up, stretching shoulders and neck, drinking a glass of water, walking outside, doing 10 deep breaths.

## Common mistakes & pitfalls
- Skipping breaks because "I'm in the zone", leading to severe burnout and sluggishness by 2:00 PM.
- Working on multiple unrelated tickets during a single pomodoro cycle.
- Abandoning the technique after the first interruption instead of resetting the timer.

## Verification checklist
- [ ] Single task is clearly identified before starting the timer.
- [ ] Zero multitasking or notification checking occurs during active sprint.
- [ ] Physical screen-free breaks are observed faithfully when the timer rings.
- [ ] Sprints are tracked to provide psychological proof of progress.
