# Time-Blocking & Focus-State Calendar Defense

## When to use
Use this skill when fragmented schedules, meeting overload, and constant Slack interruptions destroy engineering flow and prevent shipping substantial technical features.

## Core principles
- **Maker's Schedule vs. Manager's Schedule (Paul Graham)**:
  - *Managers*: Days are sliced into 30-minute intervals; shifting meetings has low cognitive cost.
  - *Makers (Engineers)*: Work requires large, unbroken 3-to-4-hour blocks. A single 15-minute meeting in the middle of an afternoon destroys the entire 4-hour deep work session.
- **Proactive Time-Blocking Beats Reactive Availability**: If you don't book your calendar for deep architectural work, others will book it for low-priority status syncs.
- **Meeting Batching & "No-Meeting Days"**: Consolidate team 1:1s, standups, and syncs into dedicated days (e.g., Tuesdays and Thursdays), leaving Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays as unbroken maker days.
- **Calendar Honesty**: Schedule time for testing, code review, and postmortems; unplanned work expands to fill all gaps.

## Step-by-step workflow
1. **Audit current calendar fragmentation**: Count the number of fragmented 30-to-60-minute gaps between meetings over the past month.
2. **Establish 3-hour Deep Work Blocks**: Schedule recurring private calendar blocks (e.g., every morning 9:00 AM – 12:00 PM) titled "Focus: Deep Architecture / Code".
3. **Consolidate and batch fragmented meetings**: Move isolated 1:1s adjacent to existing meetings (e.g., immediately after team standup).
4. **Decline meetings without clear agendas**: Politely push back on meetings where your presence is not essential; request asynchronous written updates.
5. **Silence asynchronous notifications during blocks**: Close Slack, turn off email banners, and enter "Do Not Disturb" mode.

## Behavioral guidelines & dialogue examples
- **Polite Meeting Decline Script**:
  - *Meeting Request*: "Can you join this 45-minute sync to discuss the new dashboard UI ideas?"
  - *Calendar Defense Script*: "Thanks for including me! I am currently heads-down on our database migration cutover during that block. Could you share the RFC doc or Figma mock asynchronously? I will gladly leave detailed written feedback on the PR review by 4:00 PM today so we keep things moving without needing a sync."

## Common mistakes & pitfalls
- Keeping Slack open on a second monitor during "deep work", checking every notification badge.
- Accepting every calendar invite out of politeness or fear of looking uncooperative.
- Scheduling deep work blocks for 30 minutes (real deep work requires at least 90–120 minutes to achieve flow).

## Verification checklist
- [ ] At least three 3-hour unbroken deep work blocks are scheduled weekly.
- [ ] Fragmented meetings are batched into contiguous blocks or consolidated days.
- [ ] Non-essential meetings are replaced with asynchronous written reviews.
- [ ] Notifications are silenced during scheduled deep work sessions.
