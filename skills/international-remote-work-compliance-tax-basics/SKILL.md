# International Remote Work: Compliance, EOR & Tax Basics

## When to use
Use this skill when working remotely as a software engineer or contractor for international clients or companies based in foreign jurisdictions.

## Core principles
- **Tax Residency vs Source of Income**: You generally pay income taxes where you are physically resident and tax-domiciled while performing the work, not where the client is incorporated.
- **IRS Form W-8BEN / W-8BEN-E**: Non-US developers contracting for US companies must submit Form W-8BEN (individuals) or W-8BEN-E (entities) to certify foreign status and avoid 30% US statutory withholding tax.
- **Contractor vs Employee Misclassification**: If an international company dictates your exact hours, provides equipment, and prohibits other clients, local labor authorities may classify you as an employee, exposing both parties to severe penalties.
- **Employer of Record (EOR) for Full-Time Roles**: For true full-time international employment, companies should hire via an EOR (Deel, Remote, Oyster) to handle compliant local payroll, statutory benefits, and tax remittance.

## Step-by-step workflow
1. Clarify legal engagement model: B2B Independent Contractor vs EOR Employed.
2. If contracting with US clients: execute IRS Form W-8BEN / W-8BEN-E before first invoice.
3. Establish a compliant local business entity (Sole Proprietorship, LLC, or Private Limited) in your tax residence.
4. Specify payment currency (USD, EUR, GBP) and who absorbs international wire and FX conversion fees.
5. Maintain detailed accounting records, invoices, and expense receipts for annual local tax filing.
6. Consult a qualified cross-border tax professional regarding double-taxation treaties (DTAA).

## Specifics & examples
- **Cross-Border Contract Clause (FX & Currency)**:
  'All fees under this Agreement shall be billed and paid in United States Dollars (USD). Client shall bear all international wire transfer fees, intermediary banking fees, and currency conversion surcharges such that Contractor receives the net invoice amount.'
- **Form W-8BEN Core Details**:
  - Line 1: Legal Name
  - Line 5: US ITIN/SSN (usually blank for foreigners)
  - Line 6a: Foreign Tax Identifying Number (TIN/PAN/National ID in home country)
  - Part II: Claim of Tax Treaty Benefits (citing applicable treaty article for 0% withholding on independent personal services).

## Common mistakes & pitfalls
- Failing to submit Form W-8BEN, causing the US client's accounting department to withhold 30% of your earnings.
- Accepting international payments without specifying who covers international wire and FX transaction fees.
- Ignoring local VAT/GST registration thresholds on cross-border service exports.

## Verification checklist
- [ ] Form W-8BEN completed accurately with Foreign TIN and treaty claim.
- [ ] Contract explicitly assigns wire and currency conversion fees to client.
- [ ] Local business entity and tax registration compliant with domestic residency laws.
