# Transitioning from Solo Freelancing to Consulting Agency

## When to use
Use this skill when your solo client demand consistently exceeds your billable capacity, allowing you to build an agency or advisory firm.

## Core principles
- **Decoupling Income from Personal Hours**: Solo freelancing has a hard ceiling (2,000 hours/year). An agency scales through leveraged systems, subcontracted talent, and productized retainers.
- **Transition from Doer to Strategist**: Your role evolves from writing code directly to client relationship management, architecture review, and quality assurance of junior subcontractors.
- **Subcontractor Margins & Legal Agreements**: Hire specialized subcontractors under independent contractor agreements with non-solicitation clauses, billing them out at a 40%–50% gross margin.
- **Productized Consulting Services**: Package complex development into standardized, fixed-scope engagements (e.g., '3-Day AWS FinOps Audit' for \$7,500).

## Step-by-step workflow
1. Identify your agency's core repeatable competency (e.g., 'Kubernetes migration and FinOps').
2. Build a roster of 2–3 vetted freelance subcontractors with signed independent contractor agreements.
3. Shift your client positioning from 'Freelance Developer' to 'Founder & Principal Consultant'.
4. Package offerings into high-ticket fixed-scope audits and ongoing monthly retainers.
5. Oversee discovery, architecture, and code review while delegating feature execution to contractors.
6. Reinvest agency profits into marketing, case studies, and proprietary automation tooling.

## Specifics & examples
- **The 50% Agency Margin Rule**:
  - Bill client at: \$180/hour (or equivalent fixed milestone).
  - Pay senior subcontractor: \$90–\$100/hour.
  - Agency Gross Margin: \$80–\$90/hour covers sales, PM overhead, and profit.
- **Essential Subcontractor Agreement Terms**:
  - Strict Non-Solicitation (contractor cannot work directly with your client for 24 months).
  - Back-to-Back Payment Terms (pay contractor within 7 days after client pays agency).

## Common mistakes & pitfalls
- Hiring full-time employees before having consistent, recurring monthly retainer revenue.
- Failing to review contractor code, resulting in damaged agency reputation.
- Undercharging clients such that subcontractor costs wipe out all agency profits.

## Verification checklist
- [ ] Signed contractor agreements include non-solicitation and IP assignment clauses.
- [ ] Gross margins maintained at 40%–50% above subcontractor costs.
- [ ] Repeatable consulting packages defined with standardized deliverables.
