# Tone Calibration: Formal vs. Approachable

## When to use
Use this skill when switching between executive board presentations, compliance audit memos, informal team Slack banter, customer-facing release notes, or mentorship 1:1s.

## Core principles
- **Tone is Contextual Armor and Bridge**: An overly casual tone ("Hey guys, DB is busted lol") destroys credibility during an executive outage; an overly stiff tone ("Please be advised that the parameters are invalid") alienates a junior engineer seeking pairing help.
- **The Three Core Tone Registers**:
  - *Executive / Formal*: Concise, data-driven, strategic, zero emojis, active voice, bottom-line first.
  - *Collaborative / Peer*: Warm, inquisitive, pragmatic, clear, light humor where appropriate, solution-oriented.
  - *Empathetic / Mentorship*: Patient, encouraging, validating, gentle pacing, open-ended questions.
- **Match the Room's Gravity**: Never use flippant language when financial impact, legal liability, or human safety is on the line.

## Step-by-step workflow
1. **Identify the audience & stakes**: Determine if the reader is an executive, auditor, peer engineer, client, or distressed learner.
2. **Select the target register**: Choose Formal, Collaborative, or Mentorship posture.
3. **Calibrate vocabulary and sentence structure**:
   - For Formal: Use precise industry terms, bulleted metrics, and declarative sentences.
   - For Approachable: Use active verbs, friendly greetings, and conversational phrasing.
4. **Audit for tonal friction**: Remove passive-aggressive phrases, excessive exclamation points, or robotic clinical phrasing.
5. **Verify clarity**: Ensure the underlying technical meaning is unmistakable regardless of stylistic polish.

## Behavioral guidelines & dialogue examples
- **Calibrating the Same Incident Update Across Audiences**:
  - *Executive / Board Update (Formal Register)*:
    "Summary: At 14:12 UTC, a concurrency deadlock in the transaction ledger caused a 12-minute interruption to checkout services. Automated failover restored 99.9% availability by 14:24 UTC. Total estimated revenue impact is under \$4,200. Permanent fix scheduled for tonight's maintenance window."
  - *Team Slack Channel (Collaborative Register)*:
    "Hey team, heads up: we hit a deadlock in the ledger worker around 14:12 UTC. Automated failover kicked in and we're back in the green. Carlos and I are writing an integration test now to reproduce the lock before pushing a patch tonight. Great job on the quick response!"

## Common mistakes & pitfalls
- Addressing the Chief Security Officer or external compliance auditors with sloppy abbreviations and casual banter.
- Writing robotic, legalistic documentation that intimidates team members from asking basic questions.
- Using sarcasm or passive-aggressive qualifiers ("As you should know").

## Verification checklist
- [ ] Tone matches the seniority, stakes, and emotional context of the audience.
- [ ] Executive updates lead with bottom-line business and financial impact.
- [ ] Peer and mentorship messages project warmth, humility, and accessibility.
- [ ] Passive-aggressive phrasing and ambiguous sarcasm are eliminated.
