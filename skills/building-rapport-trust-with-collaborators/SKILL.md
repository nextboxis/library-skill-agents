# Building Rapport & Trust with Collaborators

## When to use
Use this skill when joining a new engineering team, kicking off a cross-functional project, pairing with an unfamiliar engineer, or restoring damaged interpersonal relationships.

## Core principles
- **The Trust Equation (Maister & Green)**:
  $$	ext{Trustworthiness} = rac{	ext{Credibility} + 	ext{Reliability} + 	ext{Intimacy (Authenticity)}}{	ext{Self-Orientation}}$$
  - *Credibility*: You know your technical domain.
  - *Reliability*: You do what you say you will do, on time.
  - *Authenticity*: You are honest, vulnerable, and real.
  - *Self-Orientation*: High self-interest (credit-hogging, ego) destroys trust faster than technical errors.
- **Do the Invisible Heavy Lifting**: Trust is earned in the unglamorous trenches: writing clean documentation, triaging broken CI tests, updating tickets, and helping unblock peers.
- **Consistency Over Charm**: Being consistently dependable beats being charismatic but flaky.

## Step-by-step workflow
1. **Show up prepared and on time**: Respect other people's time as the most valuable non-renewable resource.
2. **Deliver on small micro-commitments**: If you promise to review a PR by 2:00 PM, review it by 1:55 PM.
3. **Listen more than you speak**: In initial meetings, ask about their systems, pain points, and historical context before prescribing new architectures.
4. **Give credit publicly and generously**: Highlight teammates' breakthroughs in standups and executive memos.
5. **Own mistakes transparently**: When your deploy causes an issue, be the first to announce it and lead the cleanup.

## Behavioral guidelines & dialogue examples
- **Dialogue Example (Building Rapport with a Guarded Legacy Maintainer)**:
  - *Arrogant Approach (Destroys Trust)*: "This codebase is ancient and full of anti-patterns. We're going to rewrite this whole service in Rust."
  - *Rapport-Building Approach (Mastery)*: "I've been reviewing this service, and I'm genuinely impressed by how resilient it has been under 5 years of traffic growth. You clearly solved some gnarly edge cases in this worker pool. I'd love to learn from your experience with these legacy constraints before we design the new event-driven pipeline."

## Common mistakes & pitfalls
- Taking credit for team accomplishments while deflecting responsibility for bugs.
- Making promises during sprint planning that you know you cannot keep.
- Gossiping about colleagues or complaining about other teams in private DMs.

## Verification checklist
- [ ] Commitments are honored reliably or renegotiated proactively before deadlines pass.
- [ ] Praise is directed outward to colleagues; responsibility for missteps is accepted cleanly.
- [ ] Collaboration is driven by team success rather than personal ego or careerism.
- [ ] Listening and curiosity precede architectural critique.
