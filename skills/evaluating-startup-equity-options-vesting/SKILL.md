# Evaluating Startup Equity: Options, RSUs & Vesting Schedules

## When to use
Use this skill when considering an offer from an early-stage startup (Seed through Series C) or pre-IPO company offering stock options or RSUs.

## Core principles
- **Shares vs Percentage Ownership**: A grant of '50,000 shares' is meaningless without knowing the Total Fully Diluted Shares. Always ask: 'What percentage of fully diluted shares does this represent?'
- **ISO vs NSO Tax Treatment**: Incentive Stock Options (ISOs) may qualify for favorable capital gains tax treatment if held for required periods, but can trigger Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT). Non-Qualified Stock Options (NSOs) trigger ordinary income tax upon exercise.
- **The 4-Year Vesting with 1-Year Cliff Standard**: 25% of your equity vests at your 12-month anniversary (the cliff); the remaining 75% vests monthly or quarterly over the subsequent 36 months.
- **Section 83(b) Election Deadline**: If purchasing early unvested stock or early-exercising options, filing an 83(b) election with the IRS within strictly 30 days is legally mandatory to avoid massive future tax liabilities.

## Step-by-step workflow
1. Request key equity figures from recruiter/founder: Number of options, Total fully diluted shares, Strike price, Preferred price per share (last round valuation).
2. Calculate current implied paper value: `(Current Fair Market Value - Strike Price) * Number of Shares`.
3. Review vesting terms: verify standard 1-year cliff / 4-year schedule and ask about Post-Termination Exercise Window (PTEW) (standard 90 days vs modern 7–10 years).
4. Analyze company runway, liquidation preferences (1x non-participating preferred), and fundraising trajectory.
5. If early exercising, consult a CPA and submit IRS Section 83(b) election via certified mail within 30 days.
6. Treat early-stage startup equity as a high-risk lottery ticket; ensure base salary meets your baseline living requirements.

## Specifics & examples
- **The 5 Critical Equity Questions for Founders**:
  1. 'What is the current total fully diluted share count of the company?'
  2. 'What was the preferred share price and post-money valuation at the last funding round?'
  3. 'What is the strike price (409A fair market value) for these common options?'
  4. 'What is the post-termination exercise window (90 days or extended)?'
  5. 'What is the current capital runway and when is the next planned fundraising event?'

## Common mistakes & pitfalls
- Accepting an equity offer without knowing the total fully diluted share count.
- Missing the strict 30-day postmark deadline for IRS Section 83(b) election.
- Assuming startup paper equity is guaranteed cash when calculating monthly living expenses.

## Verification checklist
- [ ] Total fully diluted share count and percentage ownership verified in writing.
- [ ] Post-termination exercise window (PTEW) duration confirmed.
- [ ] Tax implications (AMT, ordinary income vs capital gains) reviewed with a CPA.
