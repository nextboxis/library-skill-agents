# Narrative Arc for Presentations

## When to use
Use this skill when delivering strategic keynote pitches, venture capital fundraising decks, annual company all-hands, or major transformation proposals.

## Design principles
- **The SCQA Framework**: Build narrative tension using Situation (Context), Complication (The Problem), Question (The Strategic Challenge), and Answer (The Resolution).
- **Establish Common Ground First**: Begin with uncontroversial, validated context that all stakeholders agree with before introducing disruption.
- **Amplify the Cost of Inaction**: Frame the complication clearly—what happens to market share, revenue, or security if nothing changes?
- **Earned Climax**: Position the proposed solution as the logical, inevitable consequence of resolving the complication.

## Step-by-step workflow
1. **Act 1 - Situation (Slides 1–3)**: Establish current market conditions, growth trends, and shared organizational goals.
2. **Act 2 - Complication (Slides 4–7)**: Introduce the external disruption, bottleneck, technical debt, or competitive threat.
3. **Act 3 - The Turning Point (Slide 8)**: Introduce the core pivot, new paradigm, or breakthrough technological approach.
4. **Act 4 - The Resolution (Slides 9–14)**: Detail the execution plan, product architecture, financial model, and risk mitigations.
5. **Act 5 - The Call to Action (Slide 15)**: Close with immediate next steps, required investment, and desired timeline.

## Specifics & examples
- **12-Slide Pitch Deck Arc Outline**:
  - Slide 1: High-contrast cover with punchy thesis.
  - Slide 2: Situation: Rapid market expansion in cloud security.
  - Slide 3: Situation: Customer demand for automated incident response.
  - Slide 4: Complication: Manual SOC workflows cannot keep pace with AI threats.
  - Slide 5: Complication: Annual incident triage costs rising 65% year-over-year.
  - Slide 6: Turning Point (Divider): Introducing the Autonomous Antigravity SOC.
  - Slide 7: Resolution: Core multi-agent architecture and capability.
  - Slide 8: Resolution: Customer benchmark proof points (-82% MTTR).
  - Slide 9: Resolution: Competitive moat and patent-pending orchestration.
  - Slide 10: Resolution: Financial projections and go-to-market plan.
  - Slide 11: Resolution: Implementation roadmap and team credentials.
  - Slide 12: Call to Action: Proposed $15M Series A investment terms.

## Common mistakes
- Starting immediately with product features before establishing why the problem matters.
- Spending 80% of the presentation dwelling on the problem, leaving no time for the solution.
- Presenting a flat list of disconnected facts with no dramatic tension or momentum.

## Review checklist
- [ ] Presentation follows a clear progression: Context -> Problem -> Solution -> Action.
- [ ] The complication clearly establishes the urgency and cost of inaction.
- [ ] The solution directly addresses the specific friction points introduced in Act 2.
- [ ] Deck concludes with a decisive, unambiguous call to action.
